Executive summary

The CP AI RAG Service lets Common Platform systems upload court documents and ask questions of them, getting back grounded answers in which every claim is cited to a document and a page. It is a fully serverless Azure pipeline consumed entirely over HTTP: no SDK to adopt, no infrastructure to run, no model credentials to hold.

The service is deliberately a capability, not a product. It has no user interface of its own. You bring the screen and the users; the service brings extraction, indexing, retrieval, grounded generation, and a measured trust story underneath.

The consequence is the argument of this document: trust in the answers is not a policy statement — it is enforced in the pipeline and measured in production. A citation guard means an ungrounded answer is never returned silently, and every answer that is returned is scored.

SECTION 2

What the service does

2.1The problem it addresses

Case material accumulates faster than anyone can read it. The answer to a specific question — what did the witness actually say about the vehicle, which conditions were attached, what does the guidance require — is somewhere in the documents, but finding it is slow work, and a wrong recollection is not a small thing.

Generic chat tools answer fluently whether or not the material supports them. That failure mode is precisely the one a justice system cannot absorb. The RAG Service exists to make the opposite trade: an answer you can check beats an answer that merely sounds right.

Every claim points to a document and a page,
or the answer does not pass.
ENFORCED BY THE CITATION GUARD, NOT ASSERTED IN POLICY

Everything the service writes is grounded in retrieved passages from your own documents. The model must emit a structured fact map alongside its prose; a guard checks it; a judge model scores the result. Nothing about that is optional or bolted on — it is the shape of the pipeline.

2  What the service doesRAG Service

2.3How evidence is refined before the model sees it

Retrieval over-fetches a candidate pool of fifty chunks — vector nearest-neighbour and keyword search in one call, filtered to your metadata. Three stages then shape what the model actually reads. Each is independently configurable; together they are why answers stay dense without losing the rare fact that matters.

1

Containment dedup — information-safe by construction

Court material is full of passages duplicated across files: the same order quoted in three bundles, the same paragraph in a covering letter and its enclosure. This stage drops a chunk only when nearly all of its content already appears in a higher-ranked chunk. A copy that carries one extra crucial sentence is not a duplicate, and survives. Cross-file copies collapse; unique information never does.

2

Semantic dedup — available, off by default

A coarser cosine-similarity stage exists but ships disabled: it can drop a near-duplicate that carries unique content, which is exactly the failure the containment stage was built to avoid. It remains a toggle, not a default.

3

MMR diversification — a balanced evidence set

Maximal Marginal Relevance selects the final fifteen chunks, balancing relevance against redundancy. The model reads a smaller, more varied evidence set — fewer tokens, better coverage of the question's aspects.

2.4What an answer contains

An answer is not a paragraph of prose. The response carries the answer text with inline, human-readable source references; the full set of retrieved evidence chunks (document, page, content, your metadata) so your interface can show its working; and the query and prompt echoed back with generation timing. The citations arrive machine-readable before they are prose: the model emits a structured fact map — citation ID, document, file name, page numbers — which the service resolves, merges and rewrites into the readable form.

In the payloadWhy it is there
Answer text with inline citationsThe prose your users read; every claim carries its source and page.
Evidence chunks, optionallyDisplay the passages beside the answer, or keep them for audit. Nothing is asserted that you cannot show.
Echoed query & promptThe request is reproducible: what was asked, how the task was framed, when, and how long it took.
Status & reason (async)Pending, generated or failed — with the reason recorded when an answer was delivered degraded.
THE HONEST CAVEAT The service is single-turn: each question stands alone, with no conversation memory and no streaming. It is PDF-first, and chunk sizing is fixed service-side. These are current scoping decisions, stated here so you can design against them rather than discover them.

Built as a service

3.1The principle

The service is contract-first. A published OpenAPI specification — linted, versioned and released from its own repository — is the source of truth for every request and response shape; the service's own models are generated from it. Consumers integrate against the contract, not against our implementation, and the contract cannot drift silently because the implementation is derived from it.

3.3Processing is decoupled from serving

Every expensive step — extraction, embedding, generation, scoring — runs as a short, retryable, queue-triggered invocation on serverless compute that scales with load. The HTTP surface stays fast because it only ever validates, records and enqueues.

Duplicate deliveries and worker crashes are a designed-for condition, not an incident. A shared idempotency guard keyed on the document or transaction holds a fenced, time-limited lease over in-flight work: a redelivered message for finished work is skipped without re-running models, and a worker resumed after a crash cannot overwrite a completed answer or double-spend an LLM call. The final write is fenced by the lease's claim-time ETag — a stale worker's result is discarded, not merged.

Connecting to the service

4.1Three steps, and no more

1

Obtain an access token

Acquire an Entra ID application token for the service audience — the standard client-credentials flow every Common Platform consumer already uses.

2

Call through API Management

APIM validates the JWT on every request and injects the function-level credential; the function tier is not reachable around the gateway.

3

Call the operation you need

Five operations, stable paths, every shape in the OpenAPI contract. Point a generator at the spec and you have a typed client in any language.

4.2What you never hold

This is the part most integration conversations turn on. A consuming system needs none of the following.

  • Storage account keys or connection strings — the platform itself holds none
  • AI model endpoints, keys or deployments
  • A database, or any connection to ours
  • The retrieval pipeline, prompts or trust model
  • Any coordination with a service release
  • Capacity sized for document-scale payloads

4.3Client identity and isolation

The service is built multi-client: your client identity is threaded through every search filter, storage key, blob name, queue payload and telemetry dimension. Another client's documents are not merely hidden — a cross-client lookup resolves to 404, indistinguishable from a record that never existed. Document identifiers are unique per client, and isolation is exercised by integration tests, including header-spoofing and cross-client reads.

THE HONEST CAVEAT Client-identity enforcement is implemented and tested but sits behind a feature flag pending cut-over. Ask us where cut-over stands before you design for co-tenancy.

SECTION 5

Consuming the service

5.1The document workflow

Three calls take a PDF from a desktop to a question-ready index — one of them direct to storage, so no interface ever handles document-scale bytes.

StepCallWhat you get back
1POST /document-uploadA single-blob, short-lived SAS upload URL (create/write/read only; default 120-minute expiry) plus your document reference. Duplicate document IDs are rejected here, before any bytes move.
2PUT the upload URLThe browser or client uploads the PDF bytes directly to Blob Storage. Files over the size limit are recorded as FILE_SIZE_OVER_LIMIT and never processed.
3GET /document-upload/{documentReference}AWAITING_UPLOAD → AWAITING_INGESTION → INGESTION_SUCCESS or INGESTION_FAILED. Poll and render progress; handle the failed state.

At upload you attach your own metadata — key/value pairs stored on every chunk and filterable at query time. Metadata is the scoping mechanism: a case ID, a document class, a hearing date. The vocabulary is entirely yours.

New versions are first-class: an upload can name earlier documents it supersedes; on ingestion the old chunks are excluded from every search by an always-on filter. No consumer has to remember to exclude stale material.

5.2Asking questions

StepCallWhat you get back
APOST /answer-user-querySynchronous. The cited answer, the evidence chunks, and timing — in one round trip.
B1POST /answer-user-query-asyncAsynchronous. 202 with a transactionId; the work is queued and durable.
B2GET /answer-user-query-async-status/{transactionId}ANSWER_GENERATION_PENDING → ANSWER_GENERATED or ANSWER_GENERATION_FAILED, with the answer payload once ready — optionally including the retrieved chunks (withChunkedEntries).

Every question carries the user query, a query prompt (your task framing — who the answer is for, what register it takes), and the metadata filter scoping retrieval to your documents. You steer the framing; the grounding rules are not yours to relax.

5.3Sync or async

Synchronous, for interactive screens

One request, one cited answer — for a user waiting on the page, within your timeout budget.

Asynchronous, for everything else

Queued, durable, idempotent under retry — for callers that must never hold a request open.

5.5What ingestion does with your document

Worth knowing when you reason about answer quality, because the provenance your users see is established here.

StageHow it works
ExtractionAzure Document Intelligence layout analysis over the blob — a long-running operation; page boundaries preserved, empty pages skipped. Scanned material is read, not refused.
ChunkingRecursive splitting applied per page, so every chunk carries a page number and in-page position. Page-level citation is possible because provenance is never lost, not because it is reconstructed.

6  Trust, assurance and securityRAG Service
SECTION 6

Trust, assurance and security

6.1Citations, enforced

Answer systems are usually asked to be right. This one is additionally required to be checkable, because the reader is making a decision on the strength of it.

The fact map

The model must emit a structured map of its claims — citation ID, document, file name, page numbers — alongside the prose. The service resolves it, merges adjacent references, and rewrites markers into readable sources. Citations are data first, prose second.

The guard

An answer with no fact map, an unparseable one, or all of its markers stripped is citation-degraded. Policy decides its fate: deliver it flagged with the reason recorded, or reject it outright. A deliberate "the documents do not contain this" refusal always passes — declining is the correct grounded behaviour, and is never punished.

An ungrounded answer is never returned silently.
THE GUARD'S CONTRACT WITH EVERY CONSUMER

On the asynchronous path a degraded answer is retried afresh before the policy applies. The outcome is always explicit: a clean answer, a flagged answer with its reason in the record, or a failed transaction. Never a quiet degradation.

6.2Measured in production, not just promised

Every answer, scored online

A separate judge deployment rates every production answer's groundedness 1–5 against the very chunks it was generated from, with reasoning recorded. Scores flow to Azure Monitor as a histogram with query-type and client dimensions — your answers, observable as a distribution, per client, in production.

Every change, evaluated offline

A dedicated harness reruns the real pipeline — embed, search, generate, cite — across prompts × models × queries, reporting citation coverage, verbosity and truncation. Prompt and model changes are justified with numbers from this harness before they ship, not after.

6.3Security posture

Identity and access. API Management validates an Entra ID application JWT on every request and injects the function credential; the function tier is not reachable around the gateway. With client-identity enforcement on, a missing identity is refused with 401 and every read and write is scoped to the resolved client.

Platform and data. Storage access is managed identity only — no storage keys or connection strings exist anywhere in the configuration, and storage triggers use identity-based bindings. Inputs are hardened at the contract: schema-validated shapes, UUID constraints, metadata length caps, reserved-key rejection, and escaping of both search-filter and keyword-query inputs against injection.

A POSTURE DECISION, STATED PLAINLY Azure OpenAI content filtering is disabled by policy: the corpus is sensitive case material, and a filter that refuses to read it would fail the very users the service exists for. The trade-off is deliberate, and compensated by grounding — the model may only restate what the documents say, cited to the page.

6.4Reliability under retry

Queue redelivery is how the service heals — which means duplicate delivery is the normal case to design for, not the edge case. Three properties hold under any combination of retries, crashes and races:

  • Finished work is never redone. A duplicate delivery for a document or transaction already in a terminal state is skipped without touching the models — no double-spent extraction, embedding or generation.
  • In-flight work is never raced. A time-limited lease on the status row, claimed by conditional write, means two workers cannot process the same item concurrently; a duplicate arriving against a live lease steps back and redelivers.
  • A completed answer is never overwritten. The final write is fenced by the ETag captured when the lease was claimed. A worker resuming after a crash — whose lease was reclaimed — has its stale result discarded at the write, not merged over the finished one.

Model calls carry configurable retries and per-phase timeouts, tuned so that long generation latency is bounded deliberately rather than cut off by an accidental read timeout.

6.5Observability

Application Insights runs on every function host with requests excluded from sampling, so the trace of a request is complete, not statistical. Logging is structured throughout. Groundedness scores flow through OpenTelemetry to Azure Monitor, dimensioned by query type and client — quality is a chart someone can watch, alert on, and attribute.

7.2What is ready, and what is not

The first column is in production use today. The second is stated plainly so you can plan against it.

READY NOW
  • Five operations behind APIM with a published, linted contract
  • Sync and async answering with citations and evidence chunks
  • Citation guard with explicit degraded-answer policy
  • Groundedness scored on every production answer
  • Idempotent, crash-safe queue workers
  • Multi-client isolation implemented and integration-tested
NOT YET
  • Isolation cut-over. Enforcement is behind a feature flag pending platform cut-over.
  • Beyond PDF. One upload extension; convert at your edge for now.
  • Conversation. Single-turn only — no memory, no streaming.
  • Hard delete. Superseded documents are hidden by filter, not erased; a GDPR erasure path is platform work in flight.
  • Quality beyond groundedness. Relevance and completeness are not yet scored online.
